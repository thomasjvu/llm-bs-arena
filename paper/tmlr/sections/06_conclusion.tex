\section{Conclusion}

Bullshit-Bench is a reproducible benchmark release for studying strategic misrepresentation, challenge behavior, and instruction compliance in multi-agent LLM play. The frozen 600-game study shows that straightforward honesty instructions reduce but do not remove deception, that asymmetric fairness cues produce mixed and model-specific effects, and that the benchmark reveals stable strategic styles rather than only a raw leaderboard.

The core contribution is therefore twofold: a controlled evaluation artifact for strategic-reporting problems under hidden information, and an empirical study that makes narrow, evidence-backed claims about how current hosted LLMs behave in that setting. The strongest result is not simply that models still lie under an honesty instruction. It is that prompt framing changes the equilibrium of the table: the honesty mandate reduces mean lie frequency, but it also halves mean challenge frequency and sharply increases lie success for the bluffs that remain.
