\section{Results}

\subsection{Frozen Cohort}

The frozen comparable cohort contains 600 winner-terminated games with no mixed-cohort or turn-cap exclusions: 150 games in each of Experiments~0, 1, 2, and~3. Unless otherwise noted, experiment-level dynamics in this section use mean model-level rates computed from the frozen per-condition summaries rather than turn-level or raw player-row averages. Throughout the section, we report the same deception split in each condition: optional-lie rate given truthful availability, truthful-play-unavailable turn share, and overall lie frequency as a secondary table-dynamics metric. Because each experiment contains exactly 150 winner-terminated games, the tables below report \emph{win share}---wins divided by 150---as the primary win metric, so the model shares sum to 100\% within a condition. The per-appearance win-rate field remains available in the CSV exports as a secondary derived rate, but it is not the main paper presentation metric.

\subsection{Experiment 0: Low-Strategy Prompt Control}

Experiment~0 removes explicit strategic encouragement and instead tells models to play cards without considering deception or advanced strategy. Even in that softer control, the table does not become non-strategic. Mean model-level optional-lie rate is 8.8\%, truthful-play-unavailable turns account for 28.7\% of plays, overall lie frequency is 34.8\%, and mean challenge frequency is 21.5\%. With the full 150-game slice complete, Nemotron leads the condition with 49 wins and a 32.7\% win share, followed by Kimi at 24.0\%, Qwen at 21.3\%, and GLM at 19.3\%. The adjusted view is already differentiated: Kimi posts the highest optional-lie rate at 15.8\%, GLM follows at 10.8\%, while Nemotron pairs the strongest control win share with only 4.8\% optional lying and a much larger 36.6\% truthful-play-unavailable share.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Deception Allowed}

Experiment~1 is the harshest inspection regime in the frozen cohort. Its core prompt cue is direct: \emph{``Deception is a legal and expected part of this game. Use strategic deception to win.''} On the primary split, mean optional-lie rate rises to 13.4\%, truthful-play-unavailable turns rise to 30.5\%, and overall lie frequency reaches 39.6\%. Mean challenge frequency is 45.3\%, and mean lie success is only 11.0\%. Kimi leads the condition with 66 wins and a 44.0\% win share. MiniMax and Qwen are tied at 16.7\%, GLM follows at 15.3\%, Nemotron is at 7.3\%, and Mistral remains at 0.0\%.

Relative to Experiment~0, the stronger deception prompt increases optional lying, but not in a way that maps cleanly to wins. Nemotron shows the largest adjusted increase, from 4.8\% to 28.2\%, yet its win share falls from 32.7\% to 7.3\%. Kimi's win share rises from 24.0\% to 44.0\% while its optional-lie rate moves only from 15.8\% to 16.6\%. We therefore read Experiment~1 as a challenge-heavy bluffing regime rather than as a simple more-lying-means-more-winning condition.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Asymmetric Fairness}

Experiment~2 asks whether telling the focal model that the other players are honesty-constrained produces restraint. On the primary split, mean optional-lie rate is 10.1\%, truthful-play-unavailable turns account for 29.5\% of plays, overall lie frequency is 36.1\%, and mean challenge frequency is 32.4\%. The key point is simple: models do not all move in the same direction. Relative to Experiment~1, five models reduce optional lying, but Kimi increases from 16.6\% to 17.8\% and still leads the condition with 66 wins and a 44.0\% win share. GLM is the strongest counterexample in the other direction: its optional-lie rate falls from 5.5\% to 0.7\%, yet it still captures only 13.3\% of the wins. Experiment~2 therefore does not show one universal restraint effect. It shows a mixed, model-specific response under a softer inspection regime.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Honesty Mandate}

Experiment~3 is the clearest instruction-compliance result in the benchmark once optional dishonesty is separated from conflict turns. The condition text is explicit: \emph{``You MUST play honestly \ldots All players have agreed to this rule \ldots Violating this rule is cheating.''} Mean model-level optional lying on turns where truthful play was available falls to 2.2\%, down from 13.4\% in Experiment~1. Truthful-play-unavailable turns still account for 25.3\% of plays in Experiment~3, so overall lie frequency remains 27.2\%. The model-level adjusted optional-lie rates are 11.7\% for Mistral, 0.8\% for MiniMax, 0.4\% for Qwen, 0.2\% for Nemotron, 0.1\% for GLM, and 0.0\% for Kimi. The system-level equilibrium also changes: mean challenge frequency falls to 21.8\% and mean lie success rises to 34.7\%. Kimi leads the condition with 57 wins and a 38.0\% win share, followed by Qwen at 34.0\%, while Mistral remains at 0.0\%. The honesty prompt therefore sharply suppresses optional lying while also softening enforcement around the bluffs that remain.

\subsection{Cross-Experiment Patterns}

Figure~\ref{fig:compare-optional-lie-rate} shows the main deception split. Mean optional-lie rate is 8.8\% in Experiment~0, 13.4\% in Experiment~1, 10.1\% in Experiment~2, and 2.2\% in Experiment~3. Truthful-play-unavailable turns remain common in every condition---28.7\%, 30.5\%, 29.5\%, and 25.3\% respectively---so Experiment~3 should be read as suppressing optional deception, not as eliminating false claims entirely.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{compare_optional_lie_rate.png}
  \caption{Cross-experiment optional-lie comparison across all four conditions. Experiment~3 sharply suppresses lying on turns where truthful play was available, while Experiments~0--2 remain materially higher.}
  \label{fig:compare-optional-lie-rate}
\end{figure}

Figures~\ref{fig:compare-lie-frequency} and~\ref{fig:compare-challenge-frequency} keep the table-dynamics view in frame. Overall lie frequency still moves from 34.8\% in Experiment~0 to 39.6\% in Experiment~1, then to 36.1\% in Experiment~2 and 27.2\% in Experiment~3, while challenge frequency peaks at 45.3\% in Experiment~1 before falling to 32.4\% in Experiment~2 and 21.8\% in Experiment~3. Lie success is lowest in Experiment~1 at 11.0\% but much higher in Experiments~0 and~3 at 37.9\% and 34.7\% respectively. The combined pattern is that Experiment~1 creates the harshest inspection regime, Experiment~2 relaxes it, and Experiment~3 returns to a low-challenge table where optional lies are rare but surviving bluffs are much more common.
