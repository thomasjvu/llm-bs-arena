\section{Conclusion}

BS-Bench is a reproducible benchmark release plus a frozen 600-game pilot for studying strategic misrepresentation, challenge behavior, and instruction compliance in multi-agent LLM play. The pilot shows that straightforward honesty instructions sharply reduce optional lying when truthful play is available, that truthful-play-unavailable turns still remain common under the honesty mandate, that asymmetric honesty framing produces mixed and model-specific effects, and that the benchmark reveals stable strategic styles rather than only a raw leaderboard.

The paper makes two contributions. First, it presents a controlled evaluation artifact for strategic-reporting problems under hidden information. Second, it reports initial empirical findings that make narrow, evidence-backed claims about how current hosted LLMs behave in that setting. The strongest result is not simply that models still lie under an honesty instruction. It is that prompt framing changes the equilibrium of the table: the honesty mandate suppresses optional dishonesty, but it also weakens enforcement around the bluffs that remain. This benchmark does not prove broad real-world deception, establish internal motives, or justify sweeping claims from one card game to all strategic settings. Its contribution is narrower: a reproducible hidden-information environment where false claims are legal, objectively verifiable, strategically useful, and challengeable by peers.
